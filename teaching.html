Open Digital Methods Workshops 2026/27

Term 2026/27 Academic Year
Level Open Training Series
Instructor Jennifer Gu

A year-round series of hands-on workshops introducing web scraping, data cleaning, visualization, and AI tools for research.

The Open Digital Methods Workshops run throughout the academic year, offering hands-on, no-prior-knowledge sessions for students, faculty, and staff.

Sample sessions

  • Web scraping 101 (no-code and Python editions)
  • Data cleaning with no-code tools
  • Data visualization with Tableau
  • Fine-tuning LLMs for humanities research
  • Digital storytelling and interactive maps

Format

Sessions are 2 hours, in person or online, and open to the whole HKUST community. Watch the Events page for upcoming dates.

HUMA5630 — Digital Humanities Projects

Term Fall 2026
Level Postgraduate Course
Instructor Steve Haonan Ma

A project-based course in which students design and build digital humanities projects, from interactive maps to AI-assisted archives.

HUMA5630 Digital Humanities Projects is a project-based postgraduate course that takes students from research questions to working digital humanities outputs.

Course outline

  • Digital methods and tools: mapping, text mining, web archiving, storytelling
  • Project design: data, audience, and sustainability
  • Building a team project with mentorship from faculty and librarians
  • Presenting and publishing the finished project

Past highlights

Student projects have included interactive historical maps, digital storytelling platforms, OCR pipelines for historical scripts, and AI-assisted translation tools — many of which are showcased on this site.

Text Mining for Humanities

Term Spring 2026
Level Graduate Seminar
Instructor Gaoqi Rao

A graduate seminar on computational text analysis: corpora, distant reading, topic modelling, and LLM-assisted reading for humanistic research.

Text Mining for Humanities is a graduate seminar introducing computational approaches to the analysis of large text collections.

Topics

  • Corpus design and text encoding
  • Distant reading and visualization
  • Topic modelling and stylometry
  • Large language models as research assistants
  • Ethics and reproducibility in computational humanities

Assessment

Students complete a small text-mining project on materials from their own research area, presented in a final colloquium.
